Ten decisions behind the new Truxco homepage

Why the site uses these colours, fonts, photos and shapes. Each point shows the decision next to a live sample taken from the design.

01

A dark navy hero that builds trust

The first screen is a deep navy gradient, leaning green on one side and wine on the other. A drone photo of the real Truxco yard sits behind it at 20% opacity, with a soft shade at the bottom so the stats stay readable.

Why: buyers spend €60,000–€190,000 on a used bus. Navy reads as established and reliable, and the real yard shows the stock exists.

02

One clear promise, said once

The heading is short, in sentence case and centred: what Truxco does, and where.

Why: a visitor understands the business in about a second, before reading anything else.

03

Search comes first

The search box sits right under the heading with three fields and a green search button that fills the full height of the box.

Why: most visitors arrive to find a bus. The main task is the first thing they can do, with no hunting.

05

A small, disciplined colour system

Navy and dark grey carry the structure. Green means action. Gold is a premium accent used sparingly. Blue is kept for small labels and links. No magenta anywhere.

Why: fewer colours look more professional, and buttons stand out because nothing else competes with them.

06

Two fonts, each with one job

Barlow Bold for headings and numbers: strong and slightly condensed, like transport and vehicle brands. Inter for body text and labels: clean and readable at small sizes.

Why: the same sizes, weights and spacing are used everywhere, so every section feels like the same company.

07

Real photos instead of stock

Listings show real buses in the Truxco yard. The workshop section shows technicians, handshakes and paperwork.

Why: real photos build more trust than generic imagery and prove the in-house workshop, Truxco's biggest advantage.
08

One shape language

Containers use 16px corners, buttons inside them use 6px, and the gap between them is 10px. The navigation bar, search box, cards and buttons all follow it.

Why: matching shapes make the site feel like one system rather than parts put together.

09

Listing cards built for comparison

Every card has the same order: photo with type, badge and photo count, name, location and price, year · mileage · seats, inspection report, then two actions.

Why: operators can compare buses at a glance without opening each one.

Ready to grow, in Figma and in code

The Figma file holds a full colour system with light and dark themes, reusable components with every state (green primary, gold accent), and a locked hero. The website uses the same values.
